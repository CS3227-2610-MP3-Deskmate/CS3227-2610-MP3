# Tickets

Requesters raise tickets, Support Officers resolve them with a reply, and both roles read their own workspace's saved resolutions. The [data model and routes](../TECHNICAL-SPEC.md#data-model) and [security controls](../SECURITY-SPEC.md#tickets) have their own homes.

### RQ-1 Submit a ticket
Why: A Requester needs to ask for help in a way their workspace's Support Officers can find and answer.
- RQ-1.1 (happy) Given a signed-in Requester, when they open their role's page, then it also has a `Tickets` link to their ticket list. The list returns `200`, has the title and top-level heading `Tickets`, a `New ticket` link to the creation form and a `Requester` link back to their role's page. It shows every ticket in their workspace in creation order, earliest first, as its saved title linking to its page; if there are none, it shows `No tickets yet.` instead of ticket links.
- RQ-1.2 (happy) Given a signed-in Requester, when they open the creation form, then it returns `200` with the title and top-level heading `New ticket`, empty fields labelled `Title` and `Description`, a `Submit ticket` button and a `Tickets` link back to the list. `Title` is a single-line text field and `Description` is a multiline text field. When they submit valid values with `Submit ticket`, then exactly one ticket is saved in their workspace, open, with no replies, and the response is a `302` redirect to its page; the resulting `GET` shows its saved values, and refreshing that `GET` creates nothing.
- RQ-1.3 (happy) Given a ticket in a signed-in Requester's workspace, when they open its page, then it returns `200` with the title and top-level heading `Ticket`, the ticket's saved title and description as text, `Open` if its stored open value is true or `Closed` if false, its saved resolution reply as in RQ-3.7, and a `Tickets` link back to the list. The description's line breaks are shown as line breaks, and nothing else about it is rendered: no HTML, Markdown or links made from its text. There are no reply or resolution controls for the Requester. Opening or refreshing the page changes no ticket.
- RQ-1.4 (boundary) Given a creation submission, then `CRLF` and `CR` line endings in `Description` become `LF`, and Unicode whitespace around each field is removed before validation and saving; nothing else is normalised. `Title` must contain 1 to 200 Unicode code points and `Description` 1 to 10000 after those changes. Neither may contain a null character, and `Title` may contain no remaining `CR` or `LF`. A missing field counts as empty. When either field fails these checks, then nothing is saved and the response is `200` with the same form, retaining the raw submitted values as text except for these display-only changes: in the single-line `Title`, each `CRLF` pair and each remaining `CR` or `LF` is shown as one space; in the multiline `Description`, line endings are displayed as newlines; in either field, null characters are shown as `�`. Only fields failing these checks have the corresponding errors: `Enter a valid title (1 to 200 characters).` for `Title` and `Enter a valid description (1 to 10000 characters).` for `Description`.
- RQ-1.5 (ambiguous) Given fields that pass RQ-1.4, titles need not be unique: two tickets in one workspace may share a title. Every submission with valid fields creates a new ticket, including a resend of a successful creation. A submission that fails RQ-1.4 creates nothing.
- RQ-1.6 (adversarial) Given a request to a Requester ticket route, then the existing signed-out redirect, stored-role denial, session expiry and CSRF behavior of ACC-1.4–ACC-1.6 and ACC-1.8 applies, with the method and access order in [Routes](../TECHNICAL-SPEC.md#routes). A Support Officer gets the role-denial response before any ticket is looked up. Ticket responses use the existing cache and framing controls in [Tickets](../SECURITY-SPEC.md#tickets). No rejected request creates a ticket.
- RQ-1.7 (adversarial) Given a signed-in Requester, when they request a ticket's page outside their workspace or an identifier that does not exist, then both get the same generic `404` behavior of PLT-1.4, with no ticket title or description. The current stored workspace determines the list and every creation; extra form fields and query parameters, including a ticket identifier, workspace or role, cannot change that workspace or the ticket selected by the route. All ticket text, including saved text and invalid values shown again, is displayed as text, never as HTML or rendered Markdown.
- RQ-1.8 (failure) Given debug is off, when the database does not answer while opening the list or a page, or a database operation fails before a creation can be confirmed, then the response is the generic `500` of PLT-1.4, with no success redirect. A database-rejected or rolled-back creation leaves no new ticket. If the connection fails after the database committed, the creation may already have taken effect; reopening the list shows the stored result once the database answers. No automatic retry or recovery of unsaved form contents is promised.

Out of scope: Requesters replying, resolving or closing tickets, changing status, assigning, editing or deleting tickets; search, filtering, pagination or ordering controls, attachments, AI other than the article suggestions in RQ-5, author attribution beyond the workspace, timestamps, history or audit trails, rate limits, new dependencies, services, credentials or deployment changes, styling, which [PLT-4](platform.md#plt-4-present-every-page-consistently) covers. Support Officer reading and resolution are specified in RQ-2 and RQ-3, and article suggestions while writing a ticket are specified in RQ-5.

### RQ-2 Read workspace tickets as a Support Officer
Why: A Support Officer needs to see the requests for help in their workspace before answering them.
- RQ-2.1 (happy) Given a signed-in Support Officer, when they open their role's page, then it also has a `Tickets` link to their ticket list. The list returns `200` with the title and top-level heading `Tickets` and a `Support Officer` link back to their role's page. It shows every ticket in their current stored workspace in creation order, earliest first, as its saved title linking to its Support Officer detail page; if there are none, it shows `No tickets yet.` instead of ticket links.
- RQ-2.2 (happy) Given a ticket in a signed-in Support Officer's workspace, when they open its detail page, then it returns `200` with the title and top-level heading `Ticket`, its saved title and description, `Open` if its stored open value is true or `Closed` if false, and its saved resolution reply as in RQ-3.7. It has a `Tickets` link back to the Support Officer list.
- RQ-2.3 (ambiguous) Given tickets with the same title or different stored open values, when a Support Officer opens their list, then every ticket still appears once and each title links to that ticket's own detail page, whether open or closed. Opening or refreshing either page creates, changes and deletes no ticket. An open ticket's detail page also has a `Resolve ticket` link to the separate form in RQ-3.1; a closed ticket's detail page has no such link. RQ-2.6 applies only to the list and detail routes; the separate resolution route follows RQ-3.6. Apart from that link, neither page offers ticket creation, reply submission, status changes, assignment, editing or deletion, including a `New ticket` link. Their ticket links stay within the Support Officer ticket routes; Requester navigation and behavior remain as in RQ-1.
- RQ-2.4 (boundary) Given a signed-in Support Officer, when they open a detail page for an unknown identifier, including zero or one outside the stored identifier range defined in [Routes](../TECHNICAL-SPEC.md#routes), or for a ticket in another workspace, then each gets the same generic `404` behavior of PLT-1.4 with no ticket title or description. Leading zeros are interpreted as in Routes, so they cannot select a different ticket or workspace.
- RQ-2.5 (adversarial) Given a request to a Support Officer ticket route, then the signed-out redirect, stored-role denial and session expiry of ACC-1.4–ACC-1.6 apply before any ticket lookup: a signed-out visitor, expired session or deleted account gets `302` to exactly `/sign-in/` with no query string, and a signed-in Requester gets ACC-1.5's `403`. The current stored role and workspace are read on every request, so changes apply from the next request. Extra query parameters, form fields, headers or cookies other than the session cookie cannot grant access, select a different workspace or override the ticket identifier in the route.
- RQ-2.6 (adversarial) Given a request to either Support Officer ticket route, then only `GET` is allowed, with the CSRF, Origin, method and access order in [Routes](../TECHNICAL-SPEC.md#routes). Thus a `POST` without a valid CSRF token, or with debug off and an Origin ACC-1.8 rejects, gets ACC-1.8's `403` first; otherwise every method except `GET`, including `HEAD`, gets `405` with an empty body before access checks or ticket lookup, for either role and signed-out visitors alike. Every response carries the existing cache and framing controls in [Tickets](../SECURITY-SPEC.md#tickets), including errors and redirects. No request to these routes creates, changes or deletes a ticket.
- RQ-2.7 (adversarial) Given saved ticket text, when it appears in a Support Officer list or detail page, then the title and description are escaped plain text, never HTML or rendered Markdown. Description line breaks appear as line breaks, and text resembling a URL produces no link; the saved title in the list links only to its ticket's Support Officer detail page.
- RQ-2.8 (failure) Given debug is off, when the database does not answer or a database read fails while opening either Support Officer ticket page, including while loading the session or stored account, then the response is the generic `500` of PLT-1.4 with no ticket title or description and no success redirect. No ticket changes, and no automatic retry or new recovery behavior is promised.

Out of scope: Ticket mutations through the list or detail route (resolution uses the separate RQ-3 form), assigning tickets, Support Officers creating, editing or deleting tickets, Requester mutations, search, filtering, pagination or ordering controls, attachments, author attribution, timestamps, history or audit trails, article suggestions, quotas or rate limits, new security or recovery subsystems, new dependencies, services, credentials or deployment changes, styling, which [PLT-4](platform.md#plt-4-present-every-page-consistently) covers. AI highlighting uses the separate RQ-4 action; these list and detail routes make no AI call.

### RQ-3 Resolve a ticket with a reply
Why: A Support Officer needs to finish a request for help with an answer that their workspace's Requesters can read.
- RQ-3.1 (happy) Given an open ticket in a signed-in Support Officer's current stored workspace, when they follow its `Resolve ticket` link, then the separate resolution form returns `200` with the title and top-level heading `Resolve ticket`, the ticket's saved title and description, an empty multiline field labelled `Reply`, a `Resolve ticket` button and a `Ticket` link back to that ticket's Support Officer detail page. Opening or refreshing the form changes no ticket.
- RQ-3.2 (happy) Given an open ticket in a signed-in Support Officer's current stored workspace and a `Reply` passing RQ-3.3, when they submit `Resolve ticket`, then its normalised reply is saved and its stored open value becomes false, atomically together. Only that ticket's reply and open value change; no ticket is created or deleted. The response is a `302` redirect to that ticket's Support Officer detail page, whose resulting `GET` shows `Closed` and the saved reply. Refreshing that `GET` writes nothing; Requesters see the same saved status and reply through RQ-1.3.
- RQ-3.3 (boundary) Given a resolution submission for a ticket that is still open, then `CRLF` and `CR` line endings in `Reply` become `LF`, and surrounding Unicode whitespace is removed before validation and saving; nothing else is normalised. The result must contain 1 to 10000 Unicode code points and no null character; a missing field counts as empty. If it fails, the response is `200` with the same form and only this field error: `Enter a valid reply (1 to 10000 characters).` The raw submitted value is retained as text, with only these display changes: line endings appear as newlines and null characters appear as `�`. That submission changes no ticket.
- RQ-3.4 (ambiguous) Given a ticket that is already closed when a resolution request checks its stored status, then both `GET` and `POST` return a `302` redirect to that ticket's Support Officer detail page, without showing the resolution form, validating the submitted `Reply` or changing any ticket. This includes closed tickets whose saved reply is empty. If valid submissions race to resolve the same open ticket, at most one saves its reply and closes it; a submission overtaken by a successful resolution before saving also redirects there with `302`, without overwriting the winner's reply. Repeated or stale submissions cannot reopen the ticket, replace its reply or add another reply.
- RQ-3.5 (boundary) Given a request matching the resolution route that passes RQ-3.6, when its ticket identifier is unknown, zero, outside the stored range defined in [Routes](../TECHNICAL-SPEC.md#routes), or belongs to another workspace, then `GET` and `POST` each return the same generic `404` of PLT-1.4, before reply validation and with no ticket title, description or reply. Leading zeros name the same ticket as in Routes. A path missing the identifier segment or containing a non-ASCII-digit identifier does not match this route and gets the generic `404`, subject to the existing global CSRF and Origin checks. These requests change no ticket.
- RQ-3.6 (adversarial) Given a request matching the resolution route, then only `GET` and `POST` are allowed, with the existing CSRF, Origin, method and access order in [Routes](../TECHNICAL-SPEC.md#routes). Requests requiring CSRF and Origin checks under ACC-1.8 get its `403` first if those checks fail; otherwise an unsupported method, including `HEAD`, gets `405` with an empty body before access checks or ticket lookup. Allowed methods reuse RQ-2.5's stored-account access decision, sign-in redirect and role denial before ticket lookup or reply validation. The current stored role and workspace apply on every request; extra query parameters, form fields, headers and cookies other than the session cookie cannot override them, the route's ticket identifier, or the allowed change in RQ-3.2. No rejected request changes a ticket.
- RQ-3.7 (adversarial) Given either role's ticket detail page, a nonempty saved resolution reply appears under the heading `Reply`, with its line breaks shown as line breaks; an empty saved reply shows neither that heading nor an invented reply or placeholder, including for older closed tickets. Ticket text on the resolution form and saved or invalid reply text on any page is escaped plain text, never HTML, rendered Markdown or links made from its text. Description line breaks on the form are shown as line breaks. Every resolution-route response uses the existing cache and framing controls in [Tickets](../SECURITY-SPEC.md#tickets), including errors and redirects.
- RQ-3.8 (failure) Given debug is off, when the database does not answer or a database operation fails while opening or submitting the resolution form, including while loading the session, stored account or ticket, then the response is the generic `500` of PLT-1.4, with no ticket title, description or reply and no success redirect. A database-rejected or rolled-back resolution leaves the saved reply and open value unchanged, never saving only one of the pair. If the connection fails after the database committed, resolution may already have taken effect; reopening the ticket detail page shows the stored outcome once the database answers. No automatic retry, recovery of unsaved form contents or new recovery subsystem is promised.

Out of scope: Replying without resolving, reopening, further replies, editing or deleting a saved reply, Requesters submitting replies or resolving tickets, assignment, attribution, timestamps, history or audit trails, notifications, attachments, AI, article suggestions, search or list changes, quotas or rate limits, new security or recovery subsystems, new dependencies, services, credentials or deployment changes, styling, which [PLT-4](platform.md#plt-4-present-every-page-consistently) covers.

### RQ-4 Highlight a ticket's key sentences
Why: A Support Officer needs help spotting the important passages in a ticket's description while keeping the complete request visible.
- RQ-4.1 (happy) Given an open or closed ticket in a signed-in Support Officer's current stored workspace, when they open its detail page, then it also has a `Highlight key sentences` button submitting to the separate action in [Routes](../TECHNICAL-SPEC.md#routes). The detail GET remains GET-only and makes no AI call. An allowed submission returns `200` with the same ticket detail content, title, headings, status, saved reply, button and applicable navigation as RQ-2 and RQ-3, with selected description passages highlighted in place as RQ-4.3 specifies; the complete description and its line breaks remain visible.
- RQ-4.2 (boundary) Given a submission that passes RQ-4.5 and loads its ticket, when highlighting is requested, then exactly one call goes through the existing gateway under the [key-sentences contract](../ai/key-sentences.md#inputs-and-authorship), with the current authenticated stored user, fixed developer instructions and schema, and only the complete saved description as untrusted text. Submitted text and extra fields cannot replace it. The contract's existing gateway length and admission behavior applies without truncation, chunking or a separate feature allowance.
- RQ-4.3 (ambiguous) Given a gateway reply that passes the [output and source-selection rules](../ai/key-sentences.md#output-and-source-selection), when it is displayed, then only the accepted occurrences in the original description are highlighted, using that contract's deterministic duplicate and overlap rules. An empty selection shows `No key sentences found.` and the complete unhighlighted description. Sentence boundaries and importance are best effort; source matching establishes copied text, not semantic correctness.
- RQ-4.4 (failure) Given a loaded ticket, when the gateway returns `error` or source validation rejects any quotation, then the response is `200` with `Key sentences are unavailable. Try again later.`; when admission returns `refused`, it is `200` with `AI limit reached. Try again later.` Each shows the complete original description without highlights and preserves RQ-4.1's other content and controls, with no provider, user or failure detail in the message. Given debug is off, a database failure while loading the session, stored account or ticket before the gateway call instead gets PLT-1.4's generic `500`, with no ticket content and no gateway call.
- RQ-4.5 (adversarial) Given a request matching the separate action, then the existing CSRF, Origin, method and stored-account access order in [Routes](../TECHNICAL-SPEC.md#routes) applies: only `POST` is allowed; failed CSRF or Origin checks get ACC-1.8's `403` first, otherwise unsupported methods including `GET` and `HEAD` get an empty `405` before access checks or lookup. Allowed methods reuse RQ-2.5's current stored-role/workspace decision, session expiry, exact sign-in redirect and role denial before lookup. Unknown, foreign-workspace, zero and out-of-range identifiers share RQ-2.4's generic `404`, with the same leading-zero rule. Client values cannot override the role, workspace or route identifier. No rejected request calls the gateway.
- RQ-4.6 (adversarial) Given any highlighting result, when the page is rendered, then all description fragments remain escaped source text, with only developer-owned highlighting around the selected occurrences: no generated prose, HTML, Markdown or links from the text are rendered. The [ticket response controls](../SECURITY-SPEC.md#tickets) apply to every response, including errors and redirects. The action changes no ticket, article or user and saves no result in a session, cookie or other store; only the gateway's existing admission and audit records may change. Reopening the detail GET shows no highlight or result message and makes no AI call; explicitly resubmitting the action makes a new call. Requester pages and controls remain unchanged.

Out of scope: Summaries, generated replies, article suggestions, which RQ-5 covers while writing a ticket, guaranteed sentence segmentation or relevance, editing descriptions, title or resolution analysis, saved results, caching, retries, streaming, background work, new gateway or admission limits, models, migrations, dependencies, services, credentials, deployment changes, or new security or recovery subsystems.

### RQ-5 Suggest articles while writing a ticket
Why: A Requester needs to find their workspace's existing guidance before raising a duplicate ticket.
Where RQ-5.1–RQ-5.4 refer to the `suggestion form`, it is a `200` response with the title and top-level heading `New ticket`; fields labelled `Title` and `Description` showing the submitted values with only RQ-1.4's display-only changes; an HTML `Suggest articles` button (`<button>`), not a link, submitting to the suggestion route; the HTML `Submit ticket` button (`<button>`), not a link, submitting to the New ticket creation route and following RQ-1.2 exactly, including creating the ticket from the shown values; and a `Tickets` link back to the list. Both buttons appear in every `200` suggestion response and remain usable as RQ-1.2 and RQ-1.4 define creation. In every `200` suggestion response, the form comes first and the results area RQ-5.3 or RQ-5.4 describes comes after it.

- RQ-5.1 (happy) Given a signed-in Requester, when they open the New ticket form, then RQ-1.2's form also has an HTML `Suggest articles` button, not a link, submitting to the suggestion route; opening or refreshing the form makes no AI call. Given a submission that passes RQ-1.4 and whose gateway result is `ok` with an accepted phrase list, when they submit `Suggest articles`, then the response is `200` with the `suggestion form` followed by RQ-5.3's results area, and no ticket is created or changed.
- RQ-5.2 (boundary) Given a `Suggest articles` submission, then `Title` and `Description` are checked by RQ-1.4 exactly, before any AI work. When either fails, the response is the `suggestion form` with RQ-1.4's same per-field error messages and display-only changes, both buttons usable, no gateway call and no ticket created. When both pass, the action makes exactly one gateway call outside any database transaction, and the only untrusted text is the RQ-1.4-normalised `Title`, then two `LF` characters, then the RQ-1.4-normalised `Description`; no other field or text is sent, and it is not truncated or chunked. The [find-a-fix contract](../ai/find-a-fix.md#inputs-and-authorship) gives the gateway's 4,000 code-point cap: joined text of exactly 4,000 code points passes that cap, while text of 4,001 gets its `error` result with no provider request after admission, so the response is RQ-5.4's unavailable message and no article links. A draft whose fields pass RQ-1.4 can exceed the cap.
- RQ-5.3 (ambiguous) Given a gateway `ok`, then its reply has only a `phrases` array of at most three strings, each at most 100 Unicode code points. First reject the whole reply before any matching if any phrase is empty, and use RQ-5.4's unavailable response. An empty array is valid and shows no matching articles. Then, in the current stored workspace, casefold each remaining phrase and each article's saved title and saved body independently with Python's `str.casefold()`, with no Unicode normalisation; a non-empty phrase whose casefolded result is empty never matches. An article matches when any folded phrase is a substring of its folded title or its folded body; a match in either includes that article once, and an article appears at most once regardless of duplicate phrases or repeated occurrences. Show the earliest three matching articles by stored identity ascending, where identity is the integer primary key, never by title, query order or phrase order. After the `suggestion form`, the results area is either an `<h2>` heading `Suggested articles` followed by a `<ul>` with exactly one `<li>` per shown article and nothing else and exactly one link per `<li>`, whose text is that article's escaped saved title and whose target is exactly `/requester/articles/<id>/` with that article's stored identity; or, when the phrase array is empty or no article matches, a single `<p>` reading `No matching articles found.`, with no heading and no links. No article body, search phrase, generated text or other provider output is shown.
- RQ-5.4 (failure) Given a submission that passes RQ-1.4, when the gateway returns `error`, including because `SOC_LLM_API_KEY` is unset or empty, or the phrase list is rejected, then the response is `200` with the `suggestion form` followed by a single `<p>` reading `Article suggestions are unavailable. Try again later.`, with no `Suggested articles` heading, list, item or article link. When the gateway returns `refused`, the response has the same form and results placement with `AI limit reached. Try again later.` instead. In both, the complete `suggestion form` and both buttons remain usable. Neither response shows provider, user or failure detail. Given debug is off, when the database does not answer or a database operation fails while loading the session, stored account or articles before AI work, then the response is PLT-1.4's generic `500`, with no form fields, submitted values, buttons or suggestions and no gateway call. No automatic retry or fallback follows.
- RQ-5.5 (adversarial) Given a request matching the suggestion route, then only `POST` is allowed, with the existing CSRF, Origin, method and access order in [Routes](../TECHNICAL-SPEC.md#routes). A request needing CSRF or Origin checks under ACC-1.8 gets its existing generic `403` first; otherwise an unsupported method, including `GET` and `HEAD`, gets the existing `405` with an empty body before access checks or AI work. A signed-out visitor gets ACC-1.4's `302` redirect to `/sign-in/`, an expired session gets ACC-1.4's signed-out behavior, and a Support Officer gets ACC-1.5's existing generic `403`, all before RQ-1.4 validation or any gateway call. The generic `403` and `405`, and PLT-1.4's generic `500`, show no ticket fields, submitted values, buttons or suggestions; only the `200` responses described in RQ-5.1–RQ-5.4 hold the form, and no gateway call happens on those generic-error paths. The route has no ticket identifier. The current stored role and workspace apply on every request; extra query parameters, form fields, headers and cookies other than the session cookie cannot override them or select another workspace, article or ticket. No request creates or changes a ticket, article or user.
- RQ-5.6 (adversarial) Given any suggestion response, then submitted values and article titles are escaped plain text, never HTML, rendered Markdown or links made from their text; only the results area's article-title links use escaped saved titles and the exact `/requester/articles/<id>/` target, and no body is shown. The response uses the existing cache and framing controls in [Tickets](../SECURITY-SPEC.md#tickets), including errors and redirects. No phrase, suggestion or submitted value is retained in a session, cookie, cache or record; only the gateway's existing admission and audit records may change. Reopening the New ticket form shows no suggestions, and resubmitting `Suggest articles` makes a new call.

Out of scope: Suggestions without pressing `Suggest articles`, as the Requester types or on any ticket list or detail page; showing article bodies or generated phrases; editing or creating tickets or articles; searching articles outside the current stored workspace; ranking, relevance scoring, synonyms, stemming or fuzzy matching; saving suggestions or a suggestion history; retries, caching, streaming, background work, new gateway or admission limits, dependencies, services, credentials or deployment changes, or new security or recovery subsystems.
