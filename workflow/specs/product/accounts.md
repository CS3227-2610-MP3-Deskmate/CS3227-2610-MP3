# Accounts

How visitors get accounts and who may open which pages: anyone can create a demo workspace from the sign-in page, users sign in with a username and password, land on their own role's page, and the server refuses the other role's pages. How users, roles and workspaces are stored is in the [data model](../TECHNICAL-SPEC.md#data-model).

### ACC-1 Sign in and out by role
Why: Each user needs to reach their own role's pages, and no one may open a role's pages without being signed in with that role.
- ACC-1.1 (happy) Given a signed-out visitor, when they request `GET /sign-in/`, then the response is `200` with an HTML page whose title and top-level heading are `Sign in` and which has a `Username` field, a `Password` field and a `Sign in` button; and when they submit the username and password of an account, then they are signed in and redirected with `302` to their role's page: `/requester/` for a Requester and `/support/` for a Support Officer.
- ACC-1.2 (happy) Given a signed-in user, when they request their role's page, then the response is `200` with an HTML page whose title and top-level heading are `Requester` on `/requester/` and `Support Officer` on `/support/`, and which shows `Signed in as <username>.`, with the username shown as text and never as markup, and a `Sign out` button. When they press it, they are signed out and redirected with `302` to `/sign-in/`, and their old session cookie, even if sent again, gets the signed-out behaviour in ACC-1.4. Signing out happens only on `POST /sign-out/`, which redirects to `/sign-in/` whether or not the visitor was signed in; `GET /sign-out/` gets `405` with an empty body and leaves the user signed in.
- ACC-1.3 (failure) Given a sign-in submission that matches no account, because its username is empty, too long (more than 150 characters after trimming and NFKC normalisation as in ACC-1.7) or unknown, or its password is empty or wrong for that username, when it is submitted, then no one is signed in and the response is `200` with the sign-in page, whose only error is `Incorrect username or password.`, whose `Username` field keeps exactly what was typed, shown as text and never as markup, and whose `Password` field is empty. The response for an existing username with a wrong password is the same, apart from its CSRF token, as for an unknown username with the same submission.
- ACC-1.4 (failure) Given a visitor who is not signed in, including one whose session has ended or whose account no longer exists, when they request `GET /requester/` or `GET /support/`, then the response is a `302` redirect to exactly `/sign-in/`, with no query string. Any method other than `GET` on `/requester/` or `/support/` gets `405` with an empty body, whether the visitor is signed out or signed in with either role, unless ACC-1.8 gives `403` first. Given the database does not answer, when a visitor submits the username and password of an account or a signed-in user requests their role's page, then the response is `500` as in PLT-1.4 and the submission signs no one in.
- ACC-1.5 (adversarial) Given a signed-in Requester requesting `GET /support/`, or a signed-in Support Officer requesting `GET /requester/`, when the request arrives, then the response is `403` with an HTML page whose title and only heading are `Forbidden` and whose only other text is `This page is not for your role.`, and which shows none of the internal details listed in PLT-1.4. The server reads the user's role from their stored account on every request, so a change to the stored role applies from the next request. Nothing else the client sends, such as query parameters, form fields, headers or cookies other than the session cookie, changes which role's pages it may open, and a `next` parameter of any value on the sign-in page or in its submission, such as `https://evil.example/`, `//evil.example/` or the other role's page, does not change where sign-in redirects.
- ACC-1.6 (boundary) Given a user who signed in, when they request their role's page less than 8 hours after signing in, then it is served as in ACC-1.2, and at 8 hours or more after signing in, however active they have been, they are signed out: their role's page behaves as in ACC-1.4, and `/sign-in/` as for a signed-out visitor in ACC-1.1. The session cookie set at sign-in has no `Expires` or `Max-Age` attribute, so the browser drops it when it closes, and a session cookie the browser held before signing in no longer identifies any session once sign-in succeeds. With debug off, the session and CSRF cookies are set with `Secure` and `SameSite=Lax`, and the session cookie also with `HttpOnly`.
- ACC-1.7 (ambiguous) Given a signed-in user, when they request `/sign-in/` by `GET`, or by `POST` with a valid CSRF token, then they are redirected with `302` to their own role's page and stay signed in as the same account, even if the submission holds another account's username and password. All Unicode whitespace around a submitted username is ignored, and the rest is compared after NFKC normalisation and is otherwise case-sensitive, so `Alice` does not sign in as `alice`, but fullwidth `ａｌｉｃｅ` signs in as `alice`. Passwords match exactly, including case and spaces.
- ACC-1.8 (adversarial) Given a request by any method other than `GET`, `HEAD`, `OPTIONS` or `TRACE` to any route without a valid CSRF token, whether or not the visitor is signed in, when it arrives, then, before any other rule applies, the response is `403` with an HTML page whose title and only heading are `Forbidden` and whose only other text is `This form has expired. Go back, reload the page and try again.`, and which shows none of the internal details listed in PLT-1.4; no one is signed in or out. With debug off, such a request with a valid CSRF token gets the same `403`, also before any other rule, if its `Origin` is anything other than exactly `https://<host>` or `http://<host>` for a `<host>` listed in `DJANGO_ALLOWED_HOSTS`, whatever forwarded-scheme header, such as `X-Forwarded-Proto`, it carries: a sign-in or sign-out `POST` with a valid token and `Origin` exactly `https://<host>` is accepted, and one with `Origin: https://evil.example` gets `403`; whether one with `Origin: http://<host>` is accepted is not specified. Responses from `/sign-in/`, `/requester/` and `/support/` carry `X-Frame-Options: DENY`, and those from `/requester/` and `/support/` also carry `Cache-Control: no-store`.

Out of scope: Creating accounts or workspaces, which ACC-2 covers; resetting, changing or choosing passwords; rate limiting or locking out sign-in attempts; making failed sign-ins take equal time; which roles may open pages other than `/requester/` and `/support/`; tickets, articles and anything else a workspace holds; creating the database schema in production; a link to `/sign-in/` from `/`, which stays as PLT-1.1 describes; a message after signing out; "remember me", signing in by email or single sign-on; security headers other than those in ACC-1.8; the Django admin site; styling.

### ACC-2 Create a demo workspace
Why: There is no signup or invitation, so a visitor needs a way to get accounts of both roles, in a workspace of their own, without signing in.
- ACC-2.1 (happy) Given a signed-out visitor, when they get the sign-in page, by `GET /sign-in/` or as the response to a failed sign-in in ACC-1.3, then it also has, after the sign-in form, the text `No account? Create a demo workspace with 2 Requesters and 1 Support Officer.` and a second form whose only button is `Create demo workspace` and which sends `POST /demo-workspace/` with no field but its CSRF token. When they press it, then one new workspace is created with exactly three accounts, two Requesters and one Support Officer, and the response is `200` with an HTML page whose title and only heading are `Demo workspace created` and whose only other text is, in order: `Copy these passwords now. They will not be shown again.`; a table with the column headings `Username`, `Role` and `Password` and one row for each account, in the order of the usernames in ACC-2.3, giving its username, its role as `Requester` or `Support Officer`, and its password; and a link `Sign in` to `/sign-in/`.
- ACC-2.2 (happy) Given a workspace was just created, when each username and password its page shows is submitted on the sign-in page as in ACC-1.1, then it signs in, both Requesters to `/requester/` and the Support Officer to `/support/`. The three accounts belong to the new workspace and no other account does, and no other workspace or account changes. Creating the workspace signs no one in: afterwards, the visitor who pressed the button still gets the signed-out behaviour of ACC-1.4.
- ACC-2.3 (boundary) Given a workspace is created, then its Requesters' usernames are `requester1-<code>` and `requester2-<code>` and its Support Officer's is `support1-<code>`, where `<code>` is the same 6 characters in all three; each account's password is 16 characters and differs from the other two; and every character of a code or password is one of the 31 characters `23456789abcdefghjkmnpqrstuvwxyz`, drawn as ACC-2.8 says. A code clashes if any of its three usernames is taken when the accounts are saved, including by a creation that finished meanwhile; nothing written under a clashing code remains. When a code clashes, a new code is drawn for all three usernames, up to 3 codes in all, and if all 3 clash, creation fails as in ACC-2.4.
- ACC-2.4 (failure) Given debug is off and the database does not answer, the database fails after part of a workspace has been written, or all 3 codes clash as in ACC-2.3, when a visitor presses `Create demo workspace`, then the response is `500` as in PLT-1.4 and shows no username or password. In either debug mode, no workspace or account from the failed attempt remains.
- ACC-2.5 (adversarial) Given a request to `/demo-workspace/` without a valid CSRF token, or, with debug off, with an `Origin` that ACC-1.8 rejects, such as `https://evil.example`, when it arrives, then it gets the `403` of ACC-1.8; a request by any method other than `POST`, including `GET` and `HEAD`, that ACC-1.8 does not refuse gets `405` with an empty body; and neither creates anything. A valid `POST` that also carries form fields or query parameters, such as `username`, `password`, `role`, `workspace` or `count` with any value, creates the same as one without them: a new workspace with the three accounts of ACC-2.1, named and given passwords as in ACC-2.3, and no account in an existing workspace.
- ACC-2.6 (adversarial) Given a workspace was created, then its passwords appear only in the response to the `POST` that created it: they are stored only as Django's salted hashes and in no session, cookie or other record, and no route shows them again. Sending the same submission again, as a browser does when its user reloads the page and agrees to resend the form, creates another new workspace with different usernames and passwords and shows only those. Every response from `/demo-workspace/` carries `Cache-Control: no-store` and `X-Frame-Options: DENY`. In either debug mode, nothing the app writes to standard error or its logs, whether creation succeeds or fails as in ACC-2.4, contains a generated username, a generated password or a password hash: when creation fails, the error written as in PLT-1.4 keeps the exception type and traceback but leaves out any database message or detail containing a generated username, password or hash. Tests cover at least a duplicate username on all 3 codes of ACC-2.3, such as `Key (username)=(…) already exists.`, and a database error partway through creation whose detail holds the row being written, such as `Failing row contains (…)` with its password hash.
- ACC-2.7 (ambiguous) Given a signed-in user, when they send `POST /demo-workspace/` with a valid CSRF token, then they are redirected with `302` to their own role's page and stay signed in as the same account, and no workspace or account is created.
- ACC-2.8 (adversarial) Given a code or password is generated, then every character of it comes from the operating system's cryptographically secure random source, independently of every other character and uniformly from the 31 characters of ACC-2.3, with no biased mapping, such as reducing a random byte modulo 31. The security-reviewer checks this by reading the code. [manual]

Out of scope: Rate limiting, CAPTCHAs or other limits on creating workspaces or signing in; signup, invitations, and choosing, changing, resetting or emailing passwords; making a user change a generated password; deleting or expiring workspaces or accounts; showing the passwords again, or hiding them until the user asks; signing the visitor in on creation; sample articles, tickets or anything else a workspace holds; a link to `/sign-in/` from `/`; creating the database schema in production; styling.
