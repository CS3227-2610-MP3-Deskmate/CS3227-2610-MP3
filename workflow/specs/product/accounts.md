# Accounts

Who may open which pages: users sign in with a username and password, land on their own role's page, and the server refuses the other role's pages. How users, roles and workspaces are stored is in the [data model](../TECHNICAL-SPEC.md#data-model).

### ACC-1 Sign in and out by role
Why: Each user needs to reach their own role's pages, and no one may open a role's pages without being signed in with that role.
- ACC-1.1 (happy) Given a signed-out visitor, when they request `GET /sign-in/`, then the response is `200` with an HTML page whose title and top-level heading are `Sign in` and which has a `Username` field, a `Password` field and a `Sign in` button; and when they submit the username and password of an account, then they are signed in and redirected with `302` to their role's page: `/requester/` for a Requester and `/support/` for a Support Officer.
- ACC-1.2 (happy) Given a signed-in user, when they request their role's page, then the response is `200` with an HTML page whose title and top-level heading are `Requester` on `/requester/` and `Support Officer` on `/support/`, and which shows `Signed in as <username>.`, with the username shown as text and never as markup, and a `Sign out` button. When they press it, they are signed out and redirected with `302` to `/sign-in/`, and their old session cookie, even if sent again, gets the signed-out behaviour in ACC-1.4. Signing out happens only on `POST /sign-out/`, which redirects to `/sign-in/` whether or not the visitor was signed in; `GET /sign-out/` gets `405` with an empty body and leaves the user signed in.
- ACC-1.3 (failure) Given a sign-in submission that matches no account, because its username is empty, too long (more than 150 characters after trimming and NFKC normalisation as in ACC-1.7) or unknown, or its password is empty or wrong for that username, when it is submitted, then no one is signed in and the response is `200` with the sign-in page, whose only error is `Incorrect username or password.`, whose `Username` field keeps exactly what was typed, shown as text and never as markup, and whose `Password` field is empty. The response for an existing username with a wrong password is the same, apart from its CSRF token, as for an unknown username with the same submission.
- ACC-1.4 (failure) Given a visitor who is not signed in, including one whose session has ended or whose account no longer exists, when they request `GET /requester/` or `GET /support/`, then the response is a `302` redirect to exactly `/sign-in/`, with no query string. Any method other than `GET` on `/requester/` or `/support/` gets `405` with an empty body, whether the visitor is signed out or signed in with either role, unless ACC-1.8 gives `403` first. Given the database does not answer, when a visitor submits the username and password of an account or a signed-in user requests their role's page, then the response is `500` as in PLT-1.4 and the submission signs no one in.
- ACC-1.5 (adversarial) Given a signed-in Requester requesting `GET /support/`, or a signed-in Support Officer requesting `GET /requester/`, when the request arrives, then the response is `403` with an HTML page whose title and only heading are `Forbidden` and whose only other text is `This page is not for your role.`, and which shows none of the internal details listed in PLT-1.4. The server reads the user's role from their stored account on every request, so a change to the stored role applies from the next request. Nothing else the client sends, such as query parameters, form fields, headers or cookies other than the session cookie, changes which role's pages it may open, and a `next` parameter of any value on the sign-in page or in its submission, such as `https://evil.example/`, `//evil.example/` or the other role's page, does not change where sign-in redirects.
- ACC-1.6 (boundary) Given a user who signed in, when they request their role's page less than 8 hours after signing in, then it is served as in ACC-1.2, and at 8 hours or more after signing in, however active they have been, they are signed out: their role's page behaves as in ACC-1.4, and `/sign-in/` as for a signed-out visitor in ACC-1.1. The session cookie set at sign-in has no `Expires` or `Max-Age` attribute, so the browser drops it when it closes, and a session cookie the browser held before signing in no longer identifies any session once sign-in succeeds. With debug off, the session and CSRF cookies are set with `Secure` and `SameSite=Lax`, and the session cookie also with `HttpOnly`.
- ACC-1.7 (ambiguous) Given a signed-in user, when they request `/sign-in/` by `GET`, or by `POST` with a valid CSRF token, then they are redirected with `302` to their own role's page and stay signed in as the same account, even if the submission holds another account's username and password. All Unicode whitespace around a submitted username is ignored, and the rest is compared after NFKC normalisation and is otherwise case-sensitive, so `Alice` does not sign in as `alice`, but fullwidth `ａｌｉｃｅ` signs in as `alice`. Passwords match exactly, including case and spaces.
- ACC-1.8 (adversarial) Given a `POST`, `PUT`, `PATCH` or `DELETE` to any route without a valid CSRF token, whether or not the visitor is signed in, when it arrives, then, before any other rule applies, the response is `403` with an HTML page whose title and only heading are `Forbidden` and whose only other text is `This form has expired. Go back, reload the page and try again.`, and which shows none of the internal details listed in PLT-1.4; no one is signed in or out. With debug off, such a request with a valid CSRF token gets the same `403`, also before any other rule, if its `Origin` is anything other than exactly `https://<host>` for a `<host>` listed in `DJANGO_ALLOWED_HOSTS`, whatever forwarded-scheme header, such as `X-Forwarded-Proto`, it carries: a sign-in or sign-out `POST` with a valid token and that exact `Origin` is accepted, and one with `Origin: https://evil.example` or `Origin: http://<host>` gets `403`. Responses from `/sign-in/`, `/requester/` and `/support/` carry `X-Frame-Options: DENY`, and those from `/requester/` and `/support/` also carry `Cache-Control: no-store`.

Out of scope: Creating accounts or workspaces, including the **Create demo workspace** button; resetting, changing or choosing passwords; rate limiting or locking out sign-in attempts; making failed sign-ins take equal time; which roles may open pages other than `/requester/` and `/support/`; tickets, articles and anything else a workspace holds; creating the database schema in production; a link to `/sign-in/` from `/`, which stays as PLT-1.1 describes; a message after signing out; "remember me", signing in by email or single sign-on; security headers other than those in ACC-1.8; the Django admin site; styling.
