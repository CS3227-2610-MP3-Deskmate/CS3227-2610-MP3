# Accounts

Who may open which pages: users sign in with a username and password, land on their own role's page, and the server refuses the other role's pages. How users, roles and workspaces are stored is in the [data model](../TECHNICAL-SPEC.md#data-model).

### ACC-1 Sign in and out by role
Why: Each user needs to reach their own role's pages, and no one may open a role's pages without being signed in with that role.
- ACC-1.1 (happy) Given a signed-out visitor, when they request `GET /sign-in/`, then the response is `200` with an HTML page whose title and top-level heading are `Sign in` and which has a `Username` field, a `Password` field and a `Sign in` button; and when they submit the username and password of an account, then they are signed in and redirected with `302` to their role's page: `/requester/` for a Requester and `/support/` for a Support Officer.
- ACC-1.2 (happy) Given a signed-in user, when they request their role's page, then the response is `200` with an HTML page whose title and top-level heading are `Requester` on `/requester/` and `Support Officer` on `/support/`, and which shows `Signed in as <username>.` and a `Sign out` button. When they press it, they are signed out and redirected with `302` to `/sign-in/`, and their old session cookie, even if sent again, gets the signed-out behaviour in ACC-1.4. Signing out happens only on `POST /sign-out/`, which redirects to `/sign-in/` whether or not the visitor was signed in; `GET /sign-out/` gets `405` and leaves the user signed in.
- ACC-1.3 (failure) Given a sign-in submission that matches no account, because its username is empty, too long or unknown, or its password is empty or wrong for that username, when it is submitted, then no one is signed in and the response is `200` with the sign-in page, whose only error is `Incorrect username or password.`, whose `Username` field keeps what was typed, shown as text and never as markup, and whose `Password` field is empty. Nothing in the response shows whether the username belongs to an account.
- ACC-1.4 (failure) Given a visitor who is not signed in, including one whose session has ended or whose account no longer exists, when they request `GET /requester/` or `GET /support/`, then the response is a `302` redirect to exactly `/sign-in/`, with no query string.
- ACC-1.5 (adversarial) Given a signed-in Requester requesting `GET /support/`, or a signed-in Support Officer requesting `GET /requester/`, when the request arrives, then the response is `403` and contains none of that page's text and none of the internal details listed in PLT-1.4. The server reads the user's role from their stored account on every request, so a change to the stored role applies from the next request, and nothing else the client sends, such as query parameters, form fields, headers or cookies other than the session cookie, changes which role's pages it may open.
- ACC-1.6 (boundary) Given a user who signed in, when they request their role's page less than 8 hours after signing in, then it is served as in ACC-1.2, and when they request it 8 hours or more after signing in, however active they have been, then they get the signed-out behaviour in ACC-1.4. The session cookie set at sign-in has no `Expires` or `Max-Age` attribute, so the browser drops it when it closes.
- ACC-1.7 (ambiguous) Given a signed-in user, when they request `/sign-in/` by `GET` or `POST`, then they are redirected with `302` to their own role's page and stay signed in as the same account, even if the submission holds another account's username and password. Spaces around a submitted username are ignored, but otherwise usernames match exactly, including case, so `Alice` does not sign in as `alice`; passwords match exactly, including case and spaces.
- ACC-1.8 (adversarial) Given a `POST` to `/sign-in/` or `/sign-out/` without a valid CSRF token, when it arrives, then the response is `403` and no one is signed in or out. Given a `next` parameter of any value on the sign-in page or in its submission, such as `https://evil.example/`, `//evil.example/` or the other role's page, when sign-in succeeds, then the redirect is still to the user's own role's page. Given a session cookie the browser held before signing in, when sign-in succeeds, then that cookie's session ID no longer identifies any session. With debug off, the session and CSRF cookies are set with `Secure` and `SameSite=Lax`, and the session cookie also with `HttpOnly`.

Out of scope: Creating accounts or workspaces, including the **Create demo workspace** button; resetting, changing or choosing passwords; rate limiting or locking out sign-in attempts; which roles may open pages other than `/requester/` and `/support/`; tickets, articles and anything else a workspace holds; creating the database schema in production; a link to `/sign-in/` from `/`, which stays as PLT-1.1 describes; a message after signing out; "remember me", signing in by email or single sign-on; the Django admin site; styling.
